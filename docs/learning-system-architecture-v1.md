# TongXuan Chinese Learning System Architecture v1

**Status:** Canonical product and system design direction, recorded from the Owner's 2026-09-28 instruction. This document is a design contract, not a claim that the described engine, mastery model, content library, or learning outcomes are already implemented or empirically validated.

**Repository baseline audited:** GitHub main at 3ca711e7ec2b2e912c50630ff6466278b1a76171.

**Implementation status:** Section 12 records the inherited mainline audit. Section 14 records the Evidence Foundation v1 branch implementation; it is not a claim that the draft change has merged or deployed. Unbuilt behavior remains governed by executable code and the limitations listed there.

## 1. Product position and learning principles

TongXuan Chinese is **a Chinese learning system designed for children in the AI era**. The goal is high expectations with less wasted effort:

> 不是少學中文，而是把時間花在更重要的中文能力上。

This direction does not claim that children's abilities are declining, and it does not use a zero-pressure promise. Chinese learning still requires memory, retrieval, practice, and an appropriate degree of productive challenge.

The product principles are:

1. Memory is part of language ability; comprehension does not replace recall.
2. Recognition and active recall are separate evidence dimensions.
3. Core vocabulary should be retrievable from a concept, image, context, or approved native-language cue, not only recognizable after the Chinese answer is shown.
4. Reading a written form and recalling a spoken word from its meaning are different capabilities.
5. Spaced retrieval and delayed testing are important evidence of retention.
6. Reduce low-value repetition and put effort where the learner needs it; do not reduce the learning expectation for its own sake.
7. Handwriting remains valuable, but its requirement depends on the target and use case.
8. Interest helps sustain effective training; it does not replace academic progress.
9. Parents should eventually see evidence of learning outcomes, not only streaks or time spent.

These are Owner-directed product principles. They are not citations to a framework and do not assert that the system has passed educational validation.

## 2. Architecture boundaries and authority

The system separates the following records and decisions:

| Layer | Owns | Must not be mistaken for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curriculum authority | Approved targets, prerequisites, required dimensions, and permitted content sources | A route prototype, a lesson title list, an AI suggestion, or a child's performance history |
| Curriculum plan | An approved progression plan organized into Learning Cycles | A calendar or a sequence generated by an adaptive ranking |
| Learner state | Per-child evidence and derived status for concepts, skills, and script forms | Content authority or proof that an unapproved target may be taught |
| Progression Engine | Which eligible approved work is offered next under the time budget and learner state | The authoritative curriculum graph |
| Adaptive Lesson Player | Which practice blocks are needed for this learner and target | A source of new targets or an unconditional nine-step checklist |
| SRS | When due evidence should be retrieved again | A standalone mastery grant or curriculum sequence |
| Knowledge Station | Transfer and curiosity content within a cycle's approved language boundary | A fifth ordinary new lesson or an encyclopedia |
| Parent progress view | A read-only explanation of recorded progress and gaps | A scorer or a mutation path for learner evidence |
| School Queue | Child-specific school material and practice context | Official curriculum, graph approval, or lesson mastery |

Required authority invariants remain in force:

- AI may propose; the human Owner approves curriculum.
- Every target and content item needs claim-appropriate evidence and provenance.
- Completing one lesson never authorizes an AI to choose the next curriculum target.
- Curriculum packs and the runtime engine remain content-pack agnostic.
- Only content that passes the content-authority and rights gates may enter an approved runtime library.
- A Route 4 or other prototype may inform an experiment; it is not a curriculum authority and cannot promote its own target list.
- The audited main baseline contains proposal/validation infrastructure associated with Open Curriculum, but no Route 4 runtime or approved Route 4 curriculum authority was found. This is an audit finding, not a claim about unmerged branches.
- Third-party content is not made MIT-licensed by the repository's code license. Unknown or unauthorized material remains blocked from public publishable artifacts.

The intended offline content path is:

AI draft → deterministic validation → semantic and context review → human approval → approved content library → deterministic runtime selection.

The runtime must not ask an LLM to invent child-facing curriculum on demand.

## 3. Learner literacy and mastery evidence

The learner model is a vector of independent evidence, not one undifferentiated “knows this word” flag.

| Capability | Evidence question | Example direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HEAR | Does the learner understand heard Chinese? | Hear a known word and select or act on its meaning |
| RECALL | Can the learner retrieve Chinese from meaning? | See an approved concept/image/context cue and say the Chinese word |
| READ | Can the learner identify a written form and read it? | See a Traditional or Simplified form and produce its correct reading |
| PRODUCE | Can the learner produce the language in an appropriate form? | Speak, use phonetic/keyboard input, use voice input when supported, or handwrite when required |

The six minimum placement and progress dimensions are:

- listening;
- speaking;
- Traditional-script recognition;
- Simplified-script recognition;
- Traditional-script writing;
- Simplified-script writing.

The profile may keep additional independent dimensions such as vocabulary recall, reading, Zhuyin, Pinyin, grammar, and pronunciation. Assessment in one dimension must not silently certify another.

### 3.1 Shared lexical concept, separate orthographic state

Traditional and Simplified forms of one lexical concept share meaning, spoken-language knowledge, and grammar. They do not become two complete semantic lessons. Orthographic evidence is tracked separately for each form:

| Script | Recognition | Input | Handwriting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Traditional | Independent evidence for recognizing the Traditional form | Independent evidence for entering it | Independent handwriting evidence when required |
| Simplified | Independent evidence for recognizing the Simplified form | Independent evidence for entering it | Independent handwriting evidence when required |

The learner record must be able to represent Traditional and Simplified recognition, input, and handwriting separately. A successful Simplified attempt cannot fill a Traditional state, or the reverse. Likewise, Zhuyin/Pinyin notation practice is distinct from spoken pronunciation unless an approved scorer explicitly measures the relevant ability.

The current product direction expects high Traditional and Simplified recognition and input. Simplified handwriting carries a medium-to-high expectation because of school use; Traditional handwriting is generally medium, with higher expectations for selected high-frequency core characters. These are target-setting directions, not universal thresholds. Each approved target and assessment blueprint must state its own evidence requirement.

This section defines the target data contract only. The current placement API has general recognition and writing fields; it does not yet persist this six-part orthographic profile.

### 3.2 Handwriting expectation

Each target that involves characters must declare an expectation in its approved content contract:

| Expectation | Required learning outcome |
| --- | --- |
| WRITE_CORE | High-frequency/core characters with a strong handwriting expectation. The exact assessment and mastery evidence must be declared for the target; this label alone does not define a score. |
| WRITE_FAMILIAR | The learner has practiced the structure and can understand or identify common errors; permanent closed-book handwriting recall is not universally required. |
| READ_INPUT | Read and pronounce the form and enter it using an approved input method; full handwriting mastery is not required. |
| EXPOSURE_ONLY | Recognition or curiosity exposure only. It creates no required handwriting target or blocking gate. |

Knowledge Station terminology and incidental text normally stay at READ_INPUT or EXPOSURE_ONLY unless a reviewed, approved target says otherwise. Seeing a character does not make it a writing target.

### 3.3 Evidence and mastery contract

For each core lexical concept, the plan must specify which of the following are required: immediate recognition, immediate active recall, reading by script, spoken/typed production, handwriting expectation, and delayed retrieval. Evidence should identify the child, approved concept/target, dimension, script where applicable, prompt or cue type, result, assistance level, scorer/version, provenance, and occurrence time. Raw voice or private school media is not implied by this record and remains governed by its own privacy and rights contract.

Recognition alone does not establish active recall. A completed session does not establish mastery. A due SRS row does not establish mastery. A lesson number or curriculum position does not establish mastery. SRS supplies later retrieval opportunities and evidence; a separate approved mastery policy evaluates that evidence.

There is no single threshold asserted here for every target, script, or dimension. Thresholds, required evidence counts, delay windows, and handwriting expectations must be set in the approved target/assessment blueprint and validated before they are treated as educationally reliable.

## 4. Learning Cycle and progression authority

The basic planned structure is:

**Ordinary progression lesson 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → Knowledge Station + Cycle Check → next Learning Cycle.**

The four ordinary lessons carry the cycle's core progression. The Knowledge Station uses their approved language to revisit meaning and support cross-context transfer. The Cycle Check gathers evidence against declared requirements. Knowledge Station and Cycle Check are not a fifth ordinary new lesson.

The 4+1 structure is a curriculum shape, not five calendar days. Dates can determine due retrieval timing; they cannot authorize the next lesson or determine that a cycle has been completed. A learner may:

- complete part of a lesson in one session and resume it later;
- complete one ordinary lesson in a day;
- complete several eligible lessons when authoritative evidence supports Fast Track;
- finish a cycle at a different pace from another learner.

Only an approved curriculum plan can state which lessons belong to a cycle, which targets they introduce, and which hard prerequisites block a transition. The completed lesson number alone is not a substitute for this approved plan.

## 5. Learning-time budgets

The 10, 20, and 30 minute choices are budgets for how long the Progression Engine is allowed to run today. They do not prescribe a lesson count or a fixed number of pages.

- **10 minutes:** a minimum effective learning unit; it may complete part of a block or lesson, while a learner with strong verified mastery may progress further.
- **20 minutes:** a standard session budget.
- **30 minutes:** room for more eligible progression, retrieval, script practice, writing, SRS, or a targeted booster.

The engine may stop at a safe task boundary, persist exact progress, and continue later. It must not manufacture a completion or force an unfinished required item to pass because the clock expired. Actual time may be shorter than the selected budget. A learner may continue into more eligible work only when the relevant evidence and plan allow it.

The current API and database accept 15–25 minutes with an 18-minute default. That is an implementation contract to migrate; it does not override the 10/20/30 product direction.

## 6. Adaptive 9-Block Lesson Player

The nine-block sequence remains the ordinary full-new-lesson template:

1. 情境理解
2. 課文／對話
3. 核心生詞
4. 認識生字
5. 實用句型
6. 開口使用
7. 筆順書寫
8. 小挑戰／Exit Ticket
9. 結算 + SRS

The architecture is an **Adaptive 9-Block Lesson Player**, not a universal mandatory checklist. The learner state, approved target requirements, available time, script-specific gap, and recent errors determine whether a block is required, can be fast-tracked, can be omitted for an evidenced mastered target, or should be followed by a repair block.

The lesson package declares its target set, valid block contracts, required evidence, script coverage, handwriting expectation, and allowed repairs. The Progression Engine may select among those blocks; it cannot add a target, invent an assessment rule, or drop a blocking prerequisite without an approved contract.

Session completion, per-target mastery, cycle-check completion, and progression eligibility remain separate outcomes.

## 7. Evidence-based Fast Track

“I know this” is a request to run a diagnostic; it is not evidence and does not skip content by itself.

Fast Track must:

1. resolve the exact learner, approved targets, and target-specific evidence requirements;
2. test only the dimensions needed to establish whether instruction can be skipped;
3. record independent, assisted, incorrect, and not-assessed outcomes separately;
4. fast-track only dimensions with sufficient authoritative evidence under the approved assessment policy;
5. send unverified dimensions to the relevant block or repair;
6. preserve any required delayed retrieval or script-specific check.

Fast Track may improve throughput when a learner has already mastered much of the approved plan. It is not a self-report shortcut and does not grant permanent mastery solely from a lesson-wide score or a single quick diagnostic.

## 8. Micro Repair, Booster, and Cycle Repair

Repair is inserted when learner evidence calls for it; it is not a fixed extra lesson after every cycle.

| Repair form | Purpose | Progression effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Micro Repair | Brief in-block correction, from seconds to a few minutes | Retry the specific misunderstanding, then resume eligible work |
| Booster | Short module for a recent weak target | May be placed before or after an ordinary lesson; only its declared targets are practiced |
| Cycle Repair | Target a failed blocking prerequisite found by the Knowledge Station/Cycle Check | Repair the blocking target and re-check it with different items |

A blocking prerequisite failure may stop the dependent transition until a targeted repair and re-check succeed. A non-blocking weakness may enter SRS or a Booster while the learner continues eligible progression. A failed check does not send the learner back through every prior lesson.

## 9. Knowledge Station

Knowledge Station supports transfer and curiosity after the four ordinary progression lessons:

- reuse the cycle's approved core Chinese payload to understand a new idea;
- use new knowledge or new Chinese description as a reward and transfer opportunity;
- permit zero or a small number of new exposure-only items;
- adapt the narrative to age, prior knowledge, concept complexity, language complexity, and emotional/context appropriateness;
- seed two or three curiosity hooks across the four preceding lessons and let the child choose among them; the child chooses a topic while the system controls the curriculum;
- keep variants on the same cycle core-language payload so narrative can diverge while curriculum state reconverges;
- offer unchosen interests only as optional bonus content, never mandatory extra homework.

Possible topic pools include nature and life, science and technology, everyday knowledge, history, culture, health, arts and music, community life, and Taiwan-related life and culture. Topic diversity does not turn the Station into an encyclopedia.

The Station is not a curriculum-authority shortcut. Candidate content uses the offline approval path in Section 2 and cannot introduce unapproved core targets at runtime.

## 10. Adaptive Daily Queue and School Queue

The authoritative Daily Queue is the selection point for due review, the next eligible approved progression work, required repair, and time-budget continuation. Its output must be explainable from the approved plan and child-scoped evidence.

An Adaptive recommendation is a ranking signal. It is not the curriculum authority and is not the Daily Queue until an explicit, deterministic integration policy makes it so. It must not bypass rights, target approval, prerequisites, script gaps, or the child's selected identity.

School Queue remains a separate child-scoped school-practice source. It may help a family organize assigned material; it does not:

- promote material into the Curriculum Graph;
- become a TongXuan-authored lesson by being practiced;
- satisfy curriculum mastery without an approved linked scorer and exact target mapping;
- change the next curriculum target by itself.

Private school content keeps its privacy and provenance boundary.

## 11. First-book learning curve and parent progress

The first book should eventually have measurable listening, speaking, reading, recall, and writing KPIs. This document does not choose fixed totals or per-lesson quotas. A lesson is not required to contain 20 new characters or any other arbitrary count.

- Ordinary progression lessons introduce the approved core learning load.
- Knowledge Station primarily tests transfer and curiosity.
- SRS supports long-term retention and delayed recall.
- Learners with prior proficiency can achieve more curriculum throughput through evidence-based Fast Track.
- Reassessment should be triggered by evidence and policy, not completion of a lesson number alone.
- CCCC/TBCL may serve as external benchmark or coverage references under source policy; they do not authorize copied data or silently dictate TongXuan's sequence.

The parent view should grow toward target-level reporting for:

- newly learned core vocabulary;
- active recall and delayed retention;
- Traditional and Simplified recognition;
- reading accuracy;
- handwriting expectations met by script;
- weak and repaired targets.

Streaks and minutes may provide context but cannot stand in for learning outcomes. Current dashboards report event activity and session summaries; the target-level metrics above are not yet implemented.

## 13. Implementation checklist and recommended next PR

### Exists

- Child-scoped persisted learning sessions, exact task identities, resume/stop behavior, and authoritative task evidence.
- Separate lesson progress, assessment, points, and per-domain SRS records.
- Deterministic as-of adaptive ranking and a read-only parent activity dashboard.
- A separate School Queue provenance/lifecycle boundary.
- Source registry, rights gate, curriculum proposal validation, and manual Owner approval-record format.

### Partially exists

- Daily Queue with supported lessons and due reviews, without Learning Cycle or time-budgeted block orchestration.
- Package-defined nine-step-like flows and named modes, without universal evidence-based block selection.
- Domain-specific placement, evidence, SRS, and reporting, without full lexical recall or Traditional/Simplified mastery separation.
- Targeted lesson repair and failure stop behavior, without Cycle Repair and alternate-item Cycle Check.
- Parent reports, without cumulative product KPIs.

### Not implemented after Evidence Foundation v1

- Learning Cycle and deterministic, time-budgeted Progression Engine.
- Authoritative active lexical recall, separate text-reading assessment, and Chinese keyboard-input ingestion.
- Target-specific handwriting expectations and educational evidence-sufficiency / delayed-retention thresholds.
- Evidence-sufficiency policy that gates Fast Track by target and dimension.
- Approved Knowledge Station topic library, shared-language-payload variants, and Cycle Check.
- Cycle-level blocking and non-blocking repair transitions.
- Adaptive recommendation → authoritative Daily Queue policy.
- Parent KPI report for active recall, delayed retention, script reading, and handwriting.
- Empirical child/device validation and calibrated thresholds.

## 14. Evidence Foundation v1 implementation status

This section describes the implementation in the Evidence Foundation v1 draft branch. The inherited Section 12 audit is a mainline snapshot and should not be read as evidence that the draft migration is already on `main`.

Implemented in the branch:

- SQLite schema v6/v7 adds child-scoped targets, append-only evidence events, deterministic derived facts, an additive placement profile v2, and a validated optional target handwriting-expectation label. Existing v1 placement and attempt history remain readable; there is no legacy evidence backfill.
- Exact Traditional / Simplified orthographic target scopes are separate. Cross-script lexical identity is represented only when explicitly supplied; current production adapters do not infer equivalence.
- Evidence dimensions include HEAR, RECALL, READ, INPUT, HANDWRITING, SPEAK, ORTHOGRAPHIC_RECOGNITION, and PRONUNCIATION. Existing Learning Flow audio-to-glyph recognition writes ORTHOGRAPHIC_RECOGNITION; speaking and listening lifecycle events remain NOT_ASSESSED; validated notation matching writes PRONUNCIATION; writing activity remains NOT_ASSESSED because the existing trace result is client-supplied.
- Summary, target-history, orthographic-profile, and placement-v2 routes are child scoped. Evidence has no public write route. The existing placement route remains unchanged.
- Evidence summaries expose per-dimension/script counts and read-only event facts (active recall observations, delayed correct retrievals, independent delayed correct retrievals, and incorrect/partial observations). Target records can carry an explicitly supplied, validated handwriting expectation, but this branch assigns none to curriculum targets and does not infer requirements from learner activity.
- Frontend contracts represent script-separated facts and NOT_ASSESSED. No visual UI was changed.
- Local verification is recorded in [Learner Evidence Foundation v1](learner-evidence-foundation-v1.md): backend **333 passed**, frontend **181/181**, production build and canonical import guard **PASS (38 modules)**, migration and rights checks **PASS**. Actions and independent review are pending the final pushed exact head.

Not established: active lexical-recall scoring; distinct reading and Chinese keyboard input scoring; target-level curriculum requirement policy; educational mastery thresholds; evidence-based placement/FAST_TRACK/progression; deployed OAuth behavior; real-child/device validation; or learning efficacy. See the implementation spec for the complete route contract, record mapping, downgrade limitations, and next dependency.

Once this branch is merged, the highest-value next system step is the still-missing authoritative active lexical-recall and delayed SRS integration, subject to approved targets and prompt/scorer boundaries. Progression Engine work should follow only after that ingestion contract is genuinely supported.
